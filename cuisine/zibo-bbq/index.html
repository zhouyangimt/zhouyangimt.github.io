Zibo BBQ: A City That Got Famous for Being Decent

By Zhouyang · Shanghai

2026-11-12 zibobbqshandonggrilledpork-bellystreet-food

The grill is set into the table, flush with the laminate, a rectangle of glowing charcoal about the size of a paperback. There's a slot at one end for the spent skewers. Nobody explains this to you. You either work it out or you watch the table next to yours, where a man in a work jacket is doing it with the calm of someone who has done it a thousand times.

Then the tray lands. Twenty skewers, still spitting. Pork belly, sliced thin and folded over itself like a ribbon. Beef. Chicken wings. Two things you don't recognize — flat, pale, faintly translucent — which turn out to be tendon and heart vessel, and which are, after the pork belly, the actual reason to be here.

And then the stack of pancakes. Cold, thin enough that you can see the light through the top one, still in their plastic sleeve. A bundle of whole raw scallions, roots trimmed. A shallow dish of garlic-chili sauce. A saucer of cumin, chili powder and sesame salt, mixed and waiting.

The waiter says one thing all night, and it's the only instruction you get: 卷着吃. Roll it up and eat it.

Zibo BBQ (淄博烧烤, zībó shāokǎo) isn't a dish. It's an assembly line where the customer is the last station — and the reason eleven million people got on trains to a Shandong industrial city in the spring of 2023 was to do the last bit themselves.

The fold

The pancake goes flat on your left palm. It's wheat, unleavened, pressed thin and half-cooked so it stays pliable — a 小饼 (xiǎo bǐng), and it costs almost nothing, maybe three yuan for a stack of twenty. Take a scallion, dip the fat white end in the sauce, drag it across the pancake in a stripe. Add a pinch of the dry dip. Then lift one skewer off the grill — pork belly first, always — lay the meat along the stripe, grip the pancake around it with your left hand, and pull the skewer out with your right. It slides free. Fold the bottom up, fold the sides in, and you have a parcel about the size of your thumb, hot enough to make you juggle it.

Two bites. The fat has rendered into the pancake. The scallion is still cold and sharp and slightly aggressive. Cumin hits first, then chili, then the pork, then the raw allium burn that makes you reach for beer before you've finished chewing.

That's the whole thing. That's what the trains were for.

What makes it work

The meat arrives at the table already 70 percent cooked — grilled out back over charcoal, then carried in and finished on the little grill in front of you. This is the smartest part of the design and the part every imitation gets wrong. Your pork belly is never cold, never sitting under a heat lamp going grey, and you get to char it exactly as much as you want. Some tables like it barely warmed through. Some let the edges go black and crisp, then fight over those pieces.

The pancake is doing three jobs at once. It's a heat shield, so you don't burn your mouth. It's a grease trap, so the fat that renders off the pork belly ends up in the bread instead of on your chin. And it's a sauce delivery system, so every bite gets the same ratio of meat to chili to scallion. Chinese barbecue elsewhere doesn't do this. In Xinjiang you get lamb, cumin, and a paper plate. In the northeast you get a chicken frame and your own two hands. Zibo invented the wrapper and turned eating skewers into a group project.

And it's cheap. That isn't a footnote, it's the point. A table of four eats and drinks for less than a single steak in Shanghai.

The spring of 2023

Here's the part I still find strange. In 2022, during the last ugly stretch of the pandemic, Shandong University moved about twelve thousand students to Zibo to quarantine. The city fed them, looked after them, sent them home. Nobody made a documentary about it.

The next spring, some of those students came back to say thank you. They posted videos. The videos did what videos do. By April, Zibo was the most searched word on Chinese social media, the railway had added a 烧烤专列 — a barbecue express out of Jinan — the city ran bus lines to the grill streets, and the mayor told restaurants that anyone caught gouging visitors would be shut down. Locals drove strangers around for free. It held for a whole summer, which is longer than most things hold.

I watched all of it from Shanghai, at a desk, between meetings, and I was a snob about it. Shanghai has its own grill culture and we are insufferable about it. A city getting famous for pancakes and pork belly felt like a joke the internet was playing on itself.

The enemies

The plate version. If you order "Zibo BBQ" and a plate of finished skewers arrives with no grill in the table and no pancakes, you've been sold kebabs with a story attached. Not bad food. Just not this.

The thick pancake. A tortilla, a wrap, a pita — anything you can't see light through is the wrong bread entirely. The 小饼 is thin, slightly chewy, and it steams when it meets hot fat, which is the mechanical reason the fold works. A thick bread doesn't steam. It just sits there being bread.

The sweet glaze. Zibo seasons dry — cumin, chili, salt — and lets the fat do the talking. If your meat arrives sticky and lacquered, you're eating a northeastern sweet-glaze skewer wearing a Zibo name tag. Send it back, and be rude about it.

The boiled-then-grilled shortcut. No crust, no char, grey all the way through. You can taste the shortcut. It tastes like a steamer.

The eight-yuan skewer. In Zibo, pork belly runs about two. Anywhere outside Shandong that charges eight and calls it an imported experience is charging you for the word "Zibo," not the food.

How to order

Start with 五花肉 — pork belly — and get more than you think you need, because it goes first and it goes fast. Add beef, chicken wings, and 板筋, the beef tendon that comes out gelatinous and smoky and chewy and is much better than it sounds. Then something green, because you will need it: 韭菜 skewers, or thick slabs of eggplant. Order 马步鱼 if you see it, the little halfbeak fish that grills to a crisp you can eat bones and all.

Get beer. Get it cold, in small bottles, so it never goes warm. Get a second stack of pancakes — the first one always disappears faster than the arithmetic says it should.

Then stop talking, because the first parcel is ready, and it will be better in your hand than on the grill, and everyone at the table is about to find that out at the same moment.

Zibo BBQ isn't a recipe you can follow at home. It's a table, a grill sunk into the middle of it, a stack of pancakes, and three or four people arguing about whose fold is correct. Find the people. That's the hard part. The pork belly and the fire are easy.
